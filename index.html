The mission

Why we exist
“To promote the conservation of the physical and natural environment by advancing biodiversity, and to further public education on conservation — through science, habitat, and the animals themselves.”
01

Education

Public programmes that advance understanding of biodiversity and its stewardship.

02

Research

Scientific study of endangered species and the ecosystems that sustain them.

03

Habitat

Maintenance of habitats for endangered animals and plants, wild and protected.

The animals

Four species under active study
Vulnerable

Fossa

Cryptoprocta ferox

Madagascar's largest carnivore, threatened by deforestation across its forest range.

Endangered

Aye-Aye

Daubentonia madagascariensis

A nocturnal lemur, often persecuted by superstition despite its ecological role.

Critically endangered

Radiated Tortoise

Astrochelys radiata

Poached heavily for the pet trade; among the most trafficked tortoises on earth.

Critically endangered

Silky Sifaka

Propithecus candidus

Fewer than 250 mature individuals remain in Madagascar's north-eastern rainforest.

From the field

Dispatches

Field journal
Kianjavato, MG

A day in the life of a researcher in Madagascar

Samuel David Merson, working alongside Dr Edward Louis at the Madagascar Biodiversity Partnership's Kianjavato camp, on the day a young fosa — later named Baloo — was captured, sampled, fitted with a GPS collar, and released back into Sanga Sanga forest.
02
Ongoing

Tracking fosa movement through degraded landscapes

With less than 9% of Madagascar's original forest cover remaining, our GPS-collar research follows how fosa use fragmented, human-altered terrain.

03
Ongoing

Community and partner-led conservation on the ground

Working with local communities and partner organisations to establish protected areas and reduce human–fossa conflict across Madagascar.
